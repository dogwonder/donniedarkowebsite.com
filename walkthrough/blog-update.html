An illustrated walkthrough! Because Flash is, well, Flash, and the game has a habit of sealing itself up if you say the magic words in the wrong order, I wanted a step-by-step guide that plays the whole thing start to finish: the illustrated playthrough.

Full disclosure: I built this with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code). I knew the game inside out and what I wanted – a bot that drives a real playthrough and screenshots every move – and the AI did most of the actual writing of the Playwright automation. It was a lot of back-and-forth: it would get stuck, I’d tell it which crosshair to click or which password came next, and we calibrated the thing one step at a time. So the route through the game is mine; a good chunk of the code is the machine’s.

It’s a single self-contained page – 50 steps across 10 sections, from the opening bullseye all the way to the ending in /from/the/sky/ – with a before/after screenshot for every move and the action that was taken. The entire session is recorded as video too.

  • It’s driven by Playwright steering Ruffle. The catch: Playwright can’t see into Flash, so every input is a raw canvas coordinate or a keypress, and the bot waits for the canvas to stop changing (a pixel image-diff) rather than guessing with fixed sleeps. Getting those coordinates right was the fiddly, human part – lots of squinting at screenshots and nudging numbers.
  • Ruffle renders via WebGL, which clears its buffer after compositing, so canvas.toDataURL() just returns blank frames. The screenshots are Playwright element captures clipped to the canvas region instead – renderer-agnostic and reliable.
  • Every step, screenshot and caption is generated from a single calibrated steps file, so the walkthrough rebuilds itself whenever the run is re-recorded.
  • And yes, the passwords are all in there if you get stuck: breathe, sparrow, smurf, ling ling and rose. Say them in the wrong order and the universe stays sealed.

The best thing to come out of this whole project: Alexandra Jugović – one of the original creators of the site, co-founder of the agency Hi-ReS! – saw the rebuild and got in touch.

We got to talk about the website itself: the process, the thinking, the art behind it. Hearing the story of how it was actually made, first-hand from the person who made it, was genuinely inspirational – one of those “I can’t quite believe this is happening” moments for a side project that started as me just trying to get an old Flash site running again.

She also very kindly sent over some additional files I didn’t have – so the website is now that bit more complete, and closer to the original than I could have got it on my own. Thank you, Alexandra. ❤️
